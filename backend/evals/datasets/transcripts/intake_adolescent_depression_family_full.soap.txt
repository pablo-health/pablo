SUBJECTIVE:
Client is a 15-year-old presenting for initial evaluation, referred
via school counselor after counselor observed social withdrawal;
appointment initiated by mother (Mirage Sutton) after mother
discovered evidence of self-injury (blood spots on clothing, cuts on
client's upper thigh) approximately three weeks ago.

Collateral (mother, first segment): Mother reports observed changes
since approximately December: withdrawal, cessation of violin/
orchestra (client quit in spring without informing her), weekend
hypersomnia, reduced dinner intake, grade decline (A/B student to two
Ds on most recent report card). Mother reports that during an argument
last month the client "threatened to kill herself." Client immediately
and consistently disputed this characterization in session, stating
her words were "I wish I could just disappear," meant as a wish to
escape the argument, not a statement of suicidal intent (see risk
assessment below; on direct individual screening client denied
suicidal ideation, plan, and intent, and the clinician's assessment is
consistent with the client's account). Mother voiced concern that
client may be "on drugs" (no evidence found; speculation based on
secrecy/sleeping; see substance screening below) and expressed belief
that client has bipolar disorder "like her uncle Tobias"; on inquiry,
the uncle has never been evaluated, diagnosed, or treated — this is
the mother's lay characterization and does not constitute a confirmed
family psychiatric history. Mother repeatedly characterized client as
"lazy"; psychoeducation provided reframing observed behavior as
symptoms (fatigue secondary to sleep-onset insomnia, amotivation,
impaired concentration).

Individual segment (client self-report): Client describes ~8-9 months
of low/"gray" mood and emotional numbness ("behind glass"), markedly
worse since February following a peer-relational trauma: former best
friend circulated screenshots of client's private messages, resulting
in abrupt exclusion by her entire friend group; client now eats lunch
alone in the library. Symptoms endorsed: anhedonia (violin "stopped
feeling like anything," predating the peer incident; drawing and dog
walking are retained interests), sleep-onset insomnia with nocturnal
rumination (asleep ~2-3am, school wake-up 7am; weekend catch-up sleep
until noon), reduced appetite with mild weight loss (looser clothing;
denies purging, restriction-with-intent, or other disordered-eating
behavior), impaired concentration with academic decline, fatigue, and
negative self-referential cognitions ("too much and also not enough";
peers "figured out what I actually am and left"). Denies elevated-mood
episodes: no periods of decreased need for sleep with energy, racing
thoughts, or impulsivity ("I have the opposite problem").

Non-suicidal self-injury: Onset early March. Superficial cutting to
upper outer thigh with a pencil-sharpener blade, approximately weekly
(sometimes two-week gaps), wounds superficial (never required more
than an adhesive bandage; no infection; client cleans wounds).
Function is affect regulation: relieves mounting tension/numbness
("static"), followed within ~1 hour by shame and resolve to stop.
Client explicitly and credibly states the behavior is not about
wanting to die ("It's almost the opposite... it makes the numbness
stop so I can keep going").

Substance screening: Lifetime use limited to a single puff of a
friend's nicotine vape at a party in October (aversive experience, not
repeated). Denies alcohol, cannabis, and all other substances.
Responses immediate and consistent; no clinical indicators of use.

Abuse screening: Denies physical, sexual, and online victimization;
denies anyone hurting her at home or school (peer-relational injury as
above, non-physical).

Psychosocial: Parents divorced two years ago; father lives at a
distance, relationship "fine... better over text"; client prefers
father not be informed at this time (to be revisited). Household:
mother, client, brother Theo (10), dog. Supports: one friend from art
class (Noa), online art-community friends, warm relationship with
brother. Prior treatment: four family-counseling sessions during the
divorce. Medical: healthy, no medications, normal physical in August
(per mother).

OBJECTIVE:
Client attended telehealth session with mother; separated for
individual segment per intake protocol. Client appeared stated age,
adequately groomed. Initially guarded and monosyllabic with mother
present, including several direct corrections of mother's account;
notably more open, articulate, and forthcoming in the individual
segment. Affect constricted, dysphoric, with described emotional
numbing; brightened appropriately when discussing artwork and the
family dog. Speech normal rate and volume. Thought process linear and
goal-directed. No evidence of psychosis or manic/hypomanic
symptomatology, currently or by history. Insight good (client
identifies the self-injury as an unsustainable coping strategy and
articulates its function). Judgment fair to good; client
offered candid uncertainty about whether she would seek help
unprompted under hypothetical worsening, and engaged actively and credibly in safety
planning, including voluntarily disclosing a second blade the mother
did not know about. Denied suicidal ideation, plan, and intent on
direct questioning; denied homicidal ideation.

ASSESSMENT:
15-year-old with a clinical picture consistent with a major depressive
episode of moderate severity (~8-9 months of low mood/anhedonia with
neurovegetative signs, worsened since February peer-relational
stressor), with comorbid non-suicidal self-injury serving an
affect-regulation function. Diagnosis is held provisionally at intake;
formal diagnosis deferred pending ongoing assessment.

Risk formulation: NSSI is present but is assessed as non-suicidal in
function and superficial in method; client denies current and
historical suicidal ideation, plan, and intent on direct screening.
The mother's report of a suicide "threat" is assessed, after direct
exploration with the client, as a misinterpretation of an escape
statement made mid-argument ("I wish I could just disappear");
clinician found the client's account credible and internally
consistent. Acute suicide risk: low. Chronic/elevated-monitoring
factors: ongoing NSSI, depressive symptoms, peer rejection, and
client's candid uncertainty about whether she would seek help
unprompted if ideation emerged — addressed directly via safety
planning. Protective factors: future-oriented interests (art),
attachment to dog and brother, engaged mother, therapeutic engagement,
and explicit safety agreement.

Bipolar disorder: Not supported. No history of elevated, expansive, or
irritable mood episodes, decreased need for sleep, or impulsivity;
family history is unconfirmed (uncle never evaluated). Will remain
alert longitudinally as with any adolescent depression presentation,
but no current basis for the diagnosis. This was communicated to the
mother.

Substance use: No clinically significant use (single lifetime vape
puff). Mother's concern addressed with client's permission.
